Pressure Washing cost guide

How much does fence pressure washing cost?

Fences are priced by the square feet of boards being cleaned. To estimate it, multiply the length of the fence by its height, and double it if you want both sides done. Wood fences need a lower pressure and more care so the boards don't splinter. Vinyl fences clean up fast with a soft wash.

🛠 Do it yourself

Do it yourself or hire a pro?

A rented or home pressure washer works well on a fence if you go easy. Use a wide fan tip, keep the wand a foot or more from the wood, and move with the grain. Too much pressure leaves fuzzy, splintered boards. On vinyl, a garden-hose sprayer with fence cleaner is often enough.

Pressure washers near you

Looking for pressure washers near you…

What's usually included

  • Plants and nearby surfaces wet down or covered
  • Cleaner applied to lift mildew, algae and dirt
  • Boards washed at a pressure safe for the material
  • A final rinse of the fence and the area around it

What changes the price

  • Size. Length times height, and both sides if you want them.
  • Material. Wood needs lower pressure and more time than vinyl.
  • Condition. Heavy mildew and gray, weathered wood take longer.
  • Access. Fences behind plants or along slopes slow the work.
  • Staining. Sealing or staining afterward is priced separately.

Ways to save

  • Measure the fence length and height before you call.
  • Ask whether the price covers one side or both.
  • If you plan to stain, ask how long to wait after washing. Wood needs to dry first.

Common questions

Will pressure washing damage my wood fence?

It can if the pressure is too high. A good pro uses a low setting and a cleaner to do most of the work.

Should I stain after washing?

Washing is the best prep for stain. Let the wood dry for a couple of days first.

Do I need both sides cleaned?

Not always. Many people clean only their side. If the other side faces a neighbor, ask them first.
