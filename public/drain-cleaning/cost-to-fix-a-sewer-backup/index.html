Drain Cleaning cost guide

How much does clearing a sewer backup cost?

A sewer backup happens when the main line from your house to the street or septic tank is blocked, so wastewater comes back up through the lowest drains. The fix is clearing the main line, usually with a motorized cable through a clean-out. The table shows the plumber's part of the job. Cleaning up sewage that has spread across floors is a separate job, often done by a water damage company.

🛠 Do it yourself

Do it yourself or hire a pro?

Clearing a main line yourself means renting a heavy drum machine and working around sewage, which most people should skip. What you can do: stop all water use, keep everyone away from the backed-up area, and find your clean-out so the plumber can start right away. Wear gloves and boots if you have to walk through it.

Plumbers near you

Looking for plumbers near you…

What's usually included

  • Clean-out found and opened, or access made through a toilet or roof vent
  • Main line cabled to clear the blockage
  • Drains run to confirm water flows out again
  • A look at what came back on the cable, such as roots, grease or wipes

What changes the price

  • Timing. Backups often happen at night or on weekends, when rates are higher.
  • Access. An easy outdoor clean-out saves time. No clean-out means more work.
  • Cause. Tree roots and broken pipe take longer than a simple clog.
  • Follow-up. A camera inspection or hydro jetting is usually priced on top.
  • Cleanup. Removing sewage and drying floors is a separate cost.

Ways to save

  • Stop running water and flushing the moment you see a backup.
  • Ask the plumber whether a camera inspection is worth it if this has happened before.
  • Call your insurance company before cleanup starts. Some policies have a sewer backup add-on.

Common questions

What are the signs of a sewer backup?

Water or sewage coming up in a floor drain, shower or tub, gurgling toilets, and several drains backing up at once.

Does homeowners insurance cover sewer backups?

Standard policies often don't. Many insurers sell a sewer backup add-on that covers damage, but not the cost of clearing the line.

How do I stop it from happening again?

Don't flush wipes or pour grease down the drain. If roots are the cause, ask about regular cleaning, hydro jetting or a camera inspection to find the weak spot.

Is sewage cleanup included?

Usually not. Plumbers clear the line. A water damage or restoration company handles cleanup of floors and walls.
