Towing and Roadside Help cost guide

How much does roadside jump start cost?

A dead battery is one of the most common roadside calls. A tow company or roadside service sends a truck with a jump pack, starts your car and checks that it keeps running. You pay a flat call-out fee, which is usually higher at night and on weekends. Many drivers already have free jump starts through insurance or an auto club.

Tow operators near you

Looking for tow operators near you…

What's usually included

  • A service truck sent to your car
  • Your battery jumped with a jump pack or cables
  • A quick check that the car starts and stays running
  • Advice on whether the battery or alternator needs a test

What changes the price

  • Timing. Nights, weekends and holidays often cost more.
  • Location. Remote areas and highways can add a travel fee.
  • Access. Cars in tight garages may need longer cables or extra time.
  • Coverage. Insurance, auto clubs and some credit cards include jump starts.

Ways to save

  • Check your car insurance, auto club, new-car warranty and credit card benefits before you pay.
  • Ask for the total price before the truck is sent.
  • If the car dies again soon after, get the battery tested. Many parts stores test it for free.

Common questions

How long does a jump start take?

Once the truck arrives, usually just a few minutes. The wait for the truck is often the longest part.

Can they jump an electric or hybrid car?

Many hybrids and electric cars have a small 12-volt battery that can be jumped. Tell the dispatcher your make and model.

What if the car won't start after a jump?

The battery may be dead for good, or the problem may be the starter or alternator. Some roadside services can replace a battery on the spot. Otherwise you may need a tow.
