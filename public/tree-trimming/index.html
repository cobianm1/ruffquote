What affects the price of tree trimming

  • Height. Taller trees need climbing gear or a bucket truck and take much longer.
  • Location. Limbs over a roof or near power lines must be roped down piece by piece.
  • Cleanup. Chipping and hauling debris adds time, fuel and dump fees.
  • Tree health. Dead or storm-damaged trees can be riskier and slower to work on.

The low end of the range is a newer tree trimmer at about $75 an hour. The high end is an experienced pro at about $140 an hour who travels to you. Many tree trimmers have a minimum charge of around $150. Prices are higher in big cities.

Common questions

When is the best time to trim trees?

Late winter, while trees are dormant, is best for most species. Dead or hazardous limbs can be removed any time.

Who trims branches near power lines?

Lines running pole to pole are usually the utility's job, often for free. The line from the pole to your house is often the homeowner's. Call your utility first.

How the prices are worked out

Each price is your hourly rate times the typical time for that job, plus supplies and driving, plus your overhead and profit, rounded up to the nearest $5. Small jobs use a $150 minimum. If you work faster or slower than the times shown, adjust your hourly rate.
