Tree Trimming cost guide

How much does tree trimming near power lines cost?

Trees growing into power lines can cause outages and fires, so they need careful trimming by crews trained to work near electricity. Utilities usually clear branches around the main lines along the street at no cost to you. The line from the pole to your house is often the homeowner's job. Expect it to cost more than trimming the same tree in an open yard.

🛠 Do it yourself

Do it yourself or hire a pro?

Don't trim branches near power lines yourself. Branches can conduct electricity, and lines can be live even when the power seems off. The safe DIY step is a phone call: ask your utility whether the lines near your tree are theirs to clear, and whether they can drop the service line to your house while a crew works.

Tree trimmers near you

Looking for tree trimmers near you…

What's usually included

  • A check of which lines are nearby and who is responsible for them
  • Branches cut back from the lines by a trained crew
  • Ropes used to lower branches away from lines and the house
  • Brush chipped and hauled away, unless you ask to keep it

What changes the price

  • Line type. Main lines along the street are usually the utility's job.
  • Tree size. Taller trees need climbing gear or a bucket truck.
  • Clearance needed. More branches near the line means more careful cuts.
  • Power shut-off. Some jobs need the utility to drop the line first.
  • Cleanup. Haul-away costs more than leaving the wood stacked.

Ways to save

  • Call your utility first. They may trim around main lines for free.
  • Hire a crew with line-clearance training and insurance.
  • Ask whether the quote includes coordinating a power shut-off if one is needed.

Common questions

Will the power company trim my tree for free?

Often, for branches near the main lines on poles. The service line from the pole to your house is usually the homeowner's responsibility, though some utilities will drop it so you can hire a crew.

Why does it cost more near power lines?

The crew needs special training, more time, and sometimes a power shut-off. The risk is higher, so the price is too.

How much clearance do lines need?

It depends on the line and your utility's rules. The utility or a line-clearance crew can tell you.
