What affects the price of mobile mechanic work

  • Parts. Oil type, brake pad quality and battery size can change the price by $50 or more.
  • Vehicle. Trucks and SUVs need more oil, bigger pads and heavier batteries.
  • Convenience. Mobile mechanics come to you, which often costs a little more than a shop.
  • Add-ons. Rotors, wiper blades and filters are usually extra.

The low end of the range is a newer mobile mechanic at about $50 an hour. The high end is an experienced pro at about $95 an hour who travels to you. Many mobile mechanics have a minimum charge of around $50. Prices are higher in big cities.

Common questions

Is a mobile mechanic more expensive than a shop?

Often about the same for simple jobs. You save the trip and the wait, and many mobile mechanics have lower overhead.

Can I supply my own parts?

Some mechanics allow it and charge labor only, but they usually won't warranty parts they didn't provide.

How the prices are worked out

Each price is your hourly rate times the typical time for that job, plus supplies and driving, plus your overhead and profit, rounded up to the nearest $5. Small jobs use a $50 minimum. If you work faster or slower than the times shown, adjust your hourly rate.

Before you post prices, check two or three local mobile mechanics on Google or Facebook. If you're well below them, you can probably charge more.
