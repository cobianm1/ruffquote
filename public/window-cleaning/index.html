What affects the price of window cleaning

  • Number of windows. Most pros price per window or per pane.
  • Height. Second and third stories need ladders or water-fed poles.
  • Inside and out. Cleaning both sides nearly doubles the time.
  • Extras. Screens, tracks and hard water stains usually cost extra.

The low end of the range is a newer window cleaner at about $30 an hour. The high end is an experienced pro at about $60 an hour who travels to you. Many window cleaners have a minimum charge of around $100. Prices are higher in big cities.

Common questions

How often should windows be cleaned?

Twice a year is common. Homes near the coast, trees or busy roads may need it more often.

Are screens included?

Often not. Many window cleaners charge a few dollars extra per screen.

How the prices are worked out

Each price is your hourly rate times the typical time for that job, plus supplies and driving, plus your overhead and profit, rounded up to the nearest $5. Small jobs use a $100 minimum. If you work faster or slower than the times shown, adjust your hourly rate.

Before you post prices, check two or three local window cleaners on Google or Facebook. If you're well below them, you can probably charge more.
